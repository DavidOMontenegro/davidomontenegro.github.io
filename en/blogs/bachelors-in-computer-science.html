Bachelor's in Computer Science

When I decided to pursue a Bachelor’s degree in Computer Science, it wasn’t a sudden change of direction, it was a deliberate step toward my engineering goals and a strategic investment from Liferay into my current job in core infrastructure documentation. I’ve always enjoyed understanding how things work, and formal study felt like the right way to deepen that knowledge and strengthen the foundation I’ve built through hands-on experience. It's also pretty important in Brazil's market, since they really value the diploma, even in the software industry.


What do I hope to learn in this course? I'm definitely excited to revisit Python as I've been getting a little rusty. I'm also interested in learning about system design and optimization. In junior-level work, big O notation seems like wasted brain space. But, especially in the AI era, knowing how to avoid technical debt and optimize for speed and memory are indispensable skills. I'm sure that I'll have plenty of opportunities to apply what I learn from this curriculum and I can't wait to graduate.


I admit that so far, the content has been pretty underwhelming. After all, the course is designed for those who have no knowledge in the subject. I'm taking it slow for now, but I'm sure that when the more interesting subjects arrive, it'll positively impact my DevRel career. I'll make sure to keep myself sharp with personal projects rather than depending entirely on the coursework for my continuous improvement.


What makes all of this even more rewarding is how it won't cost me a cent. Liferay’s education reimbursement program is the company's way of investing in its workers, pushing me to grow in my current role. Without it, I don't think I'd be able to pursue this move.


Balancing full-time work and coursework has definitely tested my time management skills, but it’s also been one of the most rewarding challenges I’ve taken on. Each subject (algorithms, data structures, or software design) adds a new layer of understanding that I can apply directly to the technologies I work with every day.


I’m grateful to work for a company that recognizes the value of continuous learning and actively supports professional development. Having that kind of encouragement makes a huge difference, and it’s something I don’t take for granted.


For everyone who's working in the field, here's some advice: Don't neglect your personal improvement and growth! You might be stable and comfortable now, but make sure you invest in yourself. I'll do my best to always stay up-to-date with paperwork to prove it.
