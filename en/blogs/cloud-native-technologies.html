Cloud-Native Technologies (Kubernetes)

Another major milestone for me was being responsible for documenting Liferay's cloud-compatible and cloud-native capabilities. This project meant understanding and explaining deployments using Kubernetes, Helm charts, Terraform manifests, and Docker images: all areas that were completely new to me at the time and completely under my responsibility.


It was a deep dive into modern cloud infrastructure, and one of the most technically rewarding projects I’ve had the chance to work on. I got to install and host a complex application like Liferay in a local Kubernetes cluster and even learn the process of hosting it on AWS. Then, I broke it down step-by-step, including all possible configurations and customizations - everything from switching your search engine to configuring auto-scaling in a GovCloud environment.


Unfortunately, most of the work I put into these docs was lost when I was let go, but you can still view parts of it in Provisioning Liferay in Kubernetes. This would probably have been my magnum opus, proving just how far I've come and how technical I am. It is a little upsetting that I was terminated a week before it was published and now I have nothing to show for it.


Ever since I learned to code, I've been so focused on back-end development, I had never given DevOps the love it deserves. But I'm sure this knowledge and experience in cloud infrastructure will be useful wherever I end up next. I'm even kind of hoping my next job involves cloud and open-source. It was a great pleasure working with Raymond Augé and Jared Gorski: two experts in engineering and really good teachers.
