Learning by Teaching

Last year, Liferay had a round of bootcamps to teach all employees and partners Liferay's new features, capabilities, and best practices. I joined as a participant along with 70 coworkers from the LATAM offices. Since I wasn't involved in the preparation of presentation of the course, I expected to spend the week absorbing new information, but it turned into something much more interactive.


Being a member of the enablement department and having extensive knowledge of Liferay, I found myself assisting the team often, by answering participant questions and troubleshooting course content when issues arose. It wasn't an official part of my role, but it came naturally. My time working closely with the different product teams and documentation had given me a practical understanding that others could benefit from. What also came naturally was the certification at the end of the bootcamp, after breezing through the final quiz on my first attempt.


This experience was a small example of a larger theme in my time at Liferay: stepping into gaps, connecting information, and keeping everyone well-informed (even outside of the documentation). These moments of collaboration and impact are what I'm most proud of, and what I hope to keep bringing to the team.


It's easy to get lost in your regular work and simply do what you're told. But that's never been my style. As a Technical Writer, I've been fortunate to turn into a sort of SME at Liferay. Being one of the first people to know of new development, and tasked to teach these new tools and capabilities to others means there's a personal benefit to my work as well. Everyone's heard that famous psychology experiment about how the best way to learn is to teach. The more you do it, the better you get at it!


It was a great moment with great speakers and I'm sure the company as a whole has really benefited from initiatives like this to keep everyone informed and up-to-date. I look forward to being increasingly inolved in the future. It's always a pleasure to work with David Nebinger and Jeffrey Handa! By the way, if you ever have any questions about Liferay or even OSGi, definitely reach out. I may know a thing or two...
