My Biggest Community Management Failure

One of my biggest interests has always been linguistics. One of my life goals is to learn 20 languages and I've even taken up conlanging as a hobby (creating your own). When I started compiling my list of languages I wanted to learn, I noticed a lot of Europe on it. Being a patriotic Brazilian citizen, I thought I should give my home country some love and learn one of the languages of its original inhabitants: Nheengatu (aka modern Tupi).


I quickly found that this would be a lot more difficult than learning French or Spanish because there are so few resources. So, being the go-getter that I am, I decided to change this. I gathered every book, video, and website I could find, but was still missing something quite important: people to practice with. This is when I had the bright idea to boost the Nheengatu-learning community with a public Discord server.


After setting up the server and publicizing it as much as I could, I had a semi-successful launch day. Around 8 people joined, so not that many. But they were all engaged and excited to meet others who shared this interest. Every day, we would get together and correct each other's exercises from the learning material. It was wonderful... for about a week. I had to skip a day to focus on my studies, and immediately everyone went silent. Turns out when you're the only person keeping everyone engaged, you can't take any breaks ever.


Years later, the server is still there. It's grown to 20 members. No one ever talks. People join, comment "Dang, another dead server" and never reappear. So what did I do wrong? A lot, actually!


  • Poor publicizing: I didn't have any social media at the time aside from Reddit. I made a post in the dead r/nheengatu subreddit announcing the Discord server, and another post in r/brasil that got around 6 upvotes. No one will join your server if they don't know it exists. I really should have tried harder to reach the Tupi-learning community.
  • Inorganic growth: I was starting at the end, trying to skip ahead to reach my goals as quickly as possible rather than thinking about what was truly best for the community. You don't start a server in order to build a community. You meet the people where they are, then once a community has already formed, you can start thinking about bringing them together like this. If I really wanted this to work, I should have been more involved in other social media channels, meeting other people who are passionate about this before launch day, guaranteeing a strong community with proof of demand.
  • Too much involvement: When I, the "community manager", was the only one posting, the only one answering, the only one keeping everybody involved, I was setting myself up for failure. That's not building a community, that's talking to yourself in front of an audience. I should have waited until there was enough of a community where questions would arise naturally, where people would create study groups out of their own free will, where I could appoint other vocal members to leadership positions. There's a big difference between managing and owning a community.
  • Too little effort: In my rush to start as quickly as possible, I ended up with a pretty rushed Discord server. This isn't the excellence I normally strive for, the roles were poorly planned, the settings were rudimentary, we barely had community guidelines. I reaped what I sowed.
  • Lack of persistence: To be fair, even though I didn't start well, all of these previous problems were fixable. But after seeing the silence that befell the land when I wasn't around, I gave up. This was game over for the group and the reason we never recovered.

In the end, what seemed like a success against all odds actually had pretty clear signs of unsustainability. Let this be a lesson to all of you: In order to have a thriving community, make sure you have something people actually want to talk about and put in the work to find the people that do. But, most importantly, if you let one bad day demotivate you, you'll never reach your long-term goals.
