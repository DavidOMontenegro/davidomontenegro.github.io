My Legacy After 2 Years

When I joined the documentation team, there was a long-standing backlog of outdated or incomplete content. Through steady effort and collaboration, I helped reduce that backlog by about 80%, all while staying on top of new feature releases. Just for clarification, 80% isn't the amount that we as a team cleared ever since I joined. 80% is only counting the issues I worked on personally within my area of responsibility.


Over time, I went from being responsible for those three initial components to five. If that doesn't sound like much, let me contextualize that number. In a team of five tech writers, where I was the only junior, I was responsible for five out of fourteen components. It was a huge responsibility, but I was a pretty quick writer, so I always managed to deliver.


During this time, I wrote dozens of articles, often accompanied by the OSGi samples I explained in an earlier article. I never missed a deadline and was becoming quite the Liferay expert. If this all sounds a bit like bragging, you'd be right. I was really good at my job and no-one can deny that. But there's an important lesson here.


A Little Friendly Competition

Near the end of my time at Liferay, the enablement department was presented with a new challenge: migrating over six thousand knowledge base articles to a new system. To give us a little head start, the leadership opted to make this into a friendly hackathon.


We were all given one day to review as many articles as we could. The goal was to improve content quality and consistency while having some fun along the way. Out of the team of 12, I ended up taking first place, narrowly beating Andrew Jardine after reviewing 55 out of the 265 articles we went through that day!


My prize was a company-branded lunchbox and a pair of socks (and bragging rights). In the end, this project really shows how small, motivated efforts can make a huge difference when tackling large-scale projects.


Conclusion

I was always a "Work smart, not hard" kind of person. But I have to be honest, no one makes this kind of impact without working smart and hard. It takes dedication and effort to grind through a backlog of old content. And it takes an intelligent workflow and prioritization to ensure you can do that while staying on top of all the new work. I'd say my trajectory at Liferay was an example of finding a system that works for you.


Two of Liferay's core values are Produce Excellence and Grow & Get Better. Often, a company's supposed values seem like a bunch of vague happy-sounding nonsense, but there's a clear connection between these two. The harder you try to produce excellence, the more you'll grow and get better at what you do. The more you grow and get better, the higher the quality of the work you'll produce.
