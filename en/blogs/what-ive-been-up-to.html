What I've Been Up To...

I realized recently that I haven’t shared much about my journey on LinkedIn. So, over the next few days, I’ll be posting a short series reflecting on my time at Liferay so far: the projects I’ve been part of, the lessons I’ve learned, and the people who’ve shaped my experience.


Two and a half years ago, I graduated from my IT course and joined Liferay as a QA Tester with dreams of one day growing into a software engineer. I was excited to start my career by getting as close to the product as possible through quality assurance and continuous improvement.


Back then, just getting this opportunity was a huge milestone. I was joining a team I deeply admired, working on a product used around the world, and stepping into the tech industry in a practical way. I still remember my first week, realizing how much I had to learn and how much I wanted to. It was clear from the beginning that the software industry encompasses a lot more than just "making apps".


I started alongside a fantastic group of colleagues ensuring our platform’s stability and reliability. Me and my two incredibly talented teammates, Rubéns Soares and Cláudio Souza, had some prior coding experience, so we were trusted to help write automated tests for the company’s cloud platform. It was my first real exposure to automation and test-driven development, and it completely changed the way I thought about software.


Working in QA gave me an appreciation for the unseen effort behind every feature: the systems that prevent bugs, the processes that keep things running smoothly, and the collaboration that turns “quality” into everyone’s responsibility. Those lessons have stuck with me ever since even as I moved on from quality assurance, because they're always present. Development isn't the same with a CI/CD pipeline ensuring no one's introducing regressions to the codebase.


It's also a bit eye-opening to see the product from a new perspective. As a programmer at heart, I was always more focused on making things work, than making sure they were user-friendly. Of course, I've always valued the quality of the job, but focusing entirely on that one aspect changes how you look at everything you produce. This is probably the most important skill I learned during that time, and it's stuck with me for my entire career since then.


I'm incredibly grateful to Liferay for giving me this opportunity at a time when I had very little work experience in the industry, helping me get my foot in the door. I'm also thankful for the trust in handing over responsibility over real projects so quickly, allowing me to be a productive member of the team. Over the next few posts, I'll be sharing some of the highlights from my time here. So if you'd like to see me brag about how far I've come, stay tuned for more!
